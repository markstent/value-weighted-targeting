ABSTRACT (plain text - paste into the abstract field)
-----------------------------------------------------
Retention incentives in online gambling are often allocated by ranking players on estimated treatment effect times lifetime value. Under a fixed budget this value-weighted rule maximises operator value, but operator value and player harm can rise together. We show that the harm exposure of any budget-exhausting rule exceeds the population mean by the covariance between treatment and harm divided by the budget, and give sufficient conditions for value-weighted ranking to make it non-negative. In a simulation where harm rises with value, the estimated value-weighted rule raises mean harm among treated players from 0.421 to 0.476 at a 30% budget and leaves a gap of +0.152 at 10%. Harm tracking value is not enough: in one design an accurate value-weighted rule falls below the population mean despite a strong harm-value correlation. A harm weight on the score lowers exposure to 0.414, close to what effect-only ranking reaches with no harm input. With estimated components it raises operator value, but not through harm information: the gain appears when harm falls with value, shrinks or vanishes when harm is independent of value and becomes a cost with accurate components, consistent with trimming the noisy upper tail of the product score. A hard cap beats the harm weight only with accurate components, and its ceiling holds only for the measure it is given. In this design, with estimated effects, ranking on the effect alone beats value weighting for every learner at budgets up to 30%; removing effect-churn moderation reverses this. All parameters are illustrative.
