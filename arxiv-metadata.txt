ABSTRACT (plain text - paste into the abstract field)
-----------------------------------------------------
Retention incentives in online gambling are often allocated by ranking players on estimated treatment effect times lifetime value. Under a fixed budget this value-weighted rule maximises operator value, but operator value and player harm can rise together. We show that the harm exposure of any budget-exhausting rule exceeds the population mean by the covariance between treatment and harm divided by the budget, and give a sufficient condition under which value-weighted ranking makes that covariance positive: harm rises with value among players with the same treatment effect. In a simulation with 200 replications of 10,000 players, value-weighted ranking raises mean harm among treated players from 0.421 to 0.476, and the sign of the gap follows the sign of the harm-value association. A harm-aware rule that multiplies the score by a harm weight brings exposure to 0.414 and, with estimated components, raises operator value from 16,446 to 19,045. A hard harm cap is optimal for a given ceiling in the population, but it beats the harm weight only with feasible-oracle components, and its ceiling holds only for the harm measure it is given. With estimated treatment effects, ranking on the effect alone yields more operator value than value weighting for every learner we test at tight and moderate budgets, so the budget at which value weighting starts to pay depends on the learner. A feasible oracle that uses only covariates reaches 73.5% of the hindsight oracle, which is the right benchmark for estimation cost. All parameters are illustrative.
